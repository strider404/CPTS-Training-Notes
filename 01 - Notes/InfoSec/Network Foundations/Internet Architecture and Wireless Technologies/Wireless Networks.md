## Wireless Networks

- **Definition**: A communication system that connects devices using *radio waves* or wireless signals, enabling data exchange without physical cables.

- **Advantages**:
    - **Mobility**: Users can move freely within the coverage area.
    - **Ease of installation**: No extensive cabling is needed.
    - **Scalability**: Easier to add new devices compared to wired networks.

- **Disadvantages**:
    - **Interference**: Signals can be disrupted by obstacles or other electronics.
    - **Security risks**: Transmissions can be intercepted without proper measures.
    - **Speed limitations**: Generally slower than wired connections.

## Wireless Router

- **Function**: A device that forwards data packets between networks, combining:
	- **Routing**: Directs data to the correct destination.
	- **Wireless Access Point**: Provides Wi-Fi coverage.

- **Components**:
	- **WAN Port**: Connects to the internet source (e.g., modem).
	- **LAN Ports**: For wired connections to local devices.
	- **Antennae**: Transmit and receive wireless signals.
	- **Processor & Memory**: Handle routing and network management.


## Mobile Hotspot

- **Function**: Allows a smartphone or other device to *share its cellular data* connection via Wi-Fi.

- **Connectivity**: Uses cellular data (4G or 5G) to connect devices to the internet.

- **Characteristics**:
	- Limited range (a few meters).
	- Can significantly drain battery.
	- Protected by a password for security.

## Cell Tower

- **Definition**: A structure with *antennas* and electronic equipment that creates a "cell" in a *cellular network*.

- **Function**: Allows mobile phones to send and receive signals within a specific geographic area.

- **Operation**: Uses *radio transmitters* and receivers, managed by Base Station Controllers (BSCs), and connected to the core network via backhaul links.

- **Categories**:
	- **Macro cells**: Large towers providing extensive coverage (kilometers), ideal for rural areas.
	- **Micro/Small cells**: Smaller installations for urban, densely populated areas, filling coverage gaps.

## Frequencies in Wireless Communications

- **Concept**: Wireless communication uses *radio waves* emitted at specific frequencies (oscillation rates, measured in Hz).

- **Common Frequency Bands**:
    - **2.4 GHz**: Used by older Wi-Fi, better *wall penetration*, prone to *interference*.
    - **5 GHz**: Used by newer Wi-Fi, *faster speeds*, *shorter range*.
    - **Cellular Bands**: For 4G (LTE) and 5G, ranging from lower (700 MHz) to higher (up to 28 GHz).

- **Characteristics and Regulation**:
	- **Lower frequencies**: Travel farther, carry less data.
	- **Higher frequencies**: Shorter range, carry more data.
	- **Congestion**: Many devices on the same frequency can cause interference.
	- **Regulation**: Government agencies (like the FCC) regulate frequency allocations to prevent interference.
